Executive Summary

Northwind has strong demand and a quality offer, but loses most of its interested traffic before a conversation ever starts. The homepage leads with the company's history rather than what it sells and for whom, so first-time visitors spend their attention orienting instead of acting. The primary call-to-action asks for effort without reducing risk, and there is no fast, automated response when someone does reach out — quotes are handled manually and often take more than a day, by which point high-intent buyers have moved on.

Three issues account for the majority of estimated lost pipeline: an unclear above-the-fold offer, the absence of same-hour lead follow-up, and a quote-request flow with avoidable friction. None require a rebuild. Addressing the top two is estimated, conservatively, to recover a meaningful share of currently-lost inquiries within one quarter.

Key Findings
High impactOffer & PositioningHigh confidence

The homepage doesn't say what you sell, for whom, in the first screen

Above the fold leads with "Family-owned since 1998" and a building photo. A first-time visitor cannot quickly tell that you build custom kitchen and built-in cabinetry for residential remodels in the region. Orientation cost this high, on the most-visited page, suppresses every downstream action.

Estimated impact: high — affects the majority of new visitors.

High impactAutomation & OpsHigh confidence

No automated response in the first hour after an inquiry

Quote requests are checked and answered manually, frequently with a delay of a day or more. For considered-purchase buyers comparing two or three providers, speed of first response is a primary differentiator.

Estimated impact: high — direct effect on close rate of existing inquiries.

Medium impactConversionMedium confidence

The quote-request form asks for too much, too early

The form requests project details, budget, address, and timeline before any trust has been established. Shortening the first step typically reduces abandonment on requests like these.

Estimated impact: medium — recovers a portion of abandoned requests.

Medium impactWebsite & FunnelMedium confidence

Slow mobile load on the gallery page

The portfolio gallery — a key trust-builder — ships full-resolution images and loads slowly on mobile, where a large share of traffic arrives.

Estimated impact: medium — affects evaluation and bounce on a high-intent page.

Low impactCustomer JourneyMedium confidence

No structured post-project follow-up

After a job completes there is no consistent request for a review or referral. A smaller near-term number, but a compounding source of reputation and repeat revenue.

Estimated impact: low near-term, compounding over time.

Strategic Roadmap
Week 1

Rewrite the above-the-fold offer

State what you build, for whom, and where, in one headline and one line. Lowest effort, highest return — do this first.

Weeks 1–2

Add same-hour automated lead response

An immediate acknowledgement on every inquiry with next steps and a booking link, so no high-intent buyer waits a day.

Weeks 2–3

Shorten the quote-request first step

Reduce the initial form to the essentials; collect detail in a follow-up. Measure abandonment before and after.

Week 4

Optimize the gallery for mobile

Compress and lazy-load portfolio images. Re-check mobile load on the key evaluation page.

Ongoing

Add post-project review & referral follow-up

A simple sequence after job completion to capture reviews and surface repeat/referral work.
